Build log / Sep 2026

Inspired by FunKey S

Sep 2026milestone

I love tiny handhelds, and I've owned a FunKey S: a keychain-sized clamshell that proved a real game console can be tiny. It became the spirit behind this project, which started with a simple question: what would that idea look like with a screen on each half?

Priorities

From day one the order was: size, then battery life, then two screens, then 2D games at full speed, then everything else. Every trade-off since has been decided by that list.

The first big change

My first plan used a quad-core chip with a small FPGA to split one video output across two screens. It worked on paper and was far too complicated for one person. I switched to a simpler chip that drives each screen directly over SPI,; no FPGA, far fewer wires through the hinge, and a board I could realistically get made.

Inspiration, not a copy

What I took from FunKey S is an attitude: make it as small as it can possibly be, then make it pleasant to use. A few of my design choices are nods to it, like closing the lid to save and a single menu key. But the chip, the circuits, the boards, the enclosure and the software are all my own, designed from scratch for two screens.

A month of iterations

By the end of September the enclosure had gone through about ten versions, each one 3D-printed or checked in a viewer for collisions. The first main board design was reviewed by a paid human expert before anything was ordered. I don't know much about hardware, so I lean on reviews and on testing everything I can.
